Correlation Analysis

Understanding how operational metrics interact with each other and with ride success.

Correlation Heatmap
FINDING

Weak Correlations, Clear Temporal Signal

No single metric strongly predicts ride success. Hour of day shows the clearest association with outcomes. Ratings cluster around 4.0 — driven by factors beyond this dataset.

Key Insight: Temporal patterns (hour of day, day of week) matter more than any single operational metric for predicting ride outcomes.

FINDING

Evening Peaks Drive Cancellations

Demand rises from 7 AM and peaks sharply 6-10 PM. Cancellation rates spike during the same evening window — riders cancel more when wait times are uncertain and traffic is worst.

The 6-10 PM evening peak is both the highest-demand AND highest-cancellation period — a critical intervention window.
Problem 1

Cancellations & Driver Earnings Volatility

How do cancellations and incomplete trips affect driver earnings stability across different times and areas?

What We Analyzed

Cancellation patterns

Examined cancellation rates across 45+ pickup areas and 24 hours. Compared earnings volatility in high vs low cancellation zones.

What We Found

Hotspot concentration

Areas like Area-27, 36, 37 show 35-37% cancellation rates. These same zones have the most volatile driver earnings — high per-trip but unpredictable income.

What To Do

Dynamic incentives

Minimum earnings guarantees in high-cancellation zones during peak hours. Improved ETA accuracy. Smarter driver allocation after cancellations.

KEY FINDING RECOMMENDATION

Cancellations Are Concentrated, Not Random — And They Hit Drivers Hardest

Cancellations spike during evening peak hours (6-10 PM) and concentrate in specific high-density zones (Areas 2, 6, 11, 17, 35, 27, 36, 37). These same pockets show the highest earnings volatility — creating a double penalty where drivers face both frequent cancellations AND unpredictable income. Action: Introduce dynamic driver incentives in these zones, strengthen matching logic for faster reassignment, and add clearer wait-time communication for riders.

Problem 2

Payment Methods & Ride Completion

How do payment methods affect completion rates, cancellations, and customer experience?

What We Analyzed

Payment impact analysis

Compared completion rates, cancellation rates, and ratings across Cash, UPI, Card, and Wallet payments.

What We Found

Digital performs better

UPI and Wallet payments show higher completion rates. Cash has slightly higher cancellation rates and more rating variability.

What To Do

Nudge to digital

Small discounts or loyalty points for UPI/Wallet. Default to digital. Investigate cash friction points in the payment flow.

KEY FINDING RECOMMENDATION

Payment Choice Actively Affects Ride Outcomes

Digital payments (UPI, cards, wallets) show higher completion rates than cash. While all methods average ~67% completion, cash transactions contribute disproportionately to cancellations. Ratings are stable across methods (~4.0). Action: Incentivize digital payments through loyalty rewards, default to UPI/Wallet, and investigate cash-related friction in the booking/trip flow.

Problem 3

Waiting Time & Service Quality

How do waiting times (VTAT/CTAT) affect cancellations and customer satisfaction?

What We Analyzed

VTAT patterns & ratings

Analyzed VTAT distribution and its relationship with customer ratings. Used time-of-day and area proxies for broader context.

What We Found

Contextual waiting effects

Direct VTAT-rating correlation is near-zero. But hourly and area-level patterns show clear links between longer waits and higher cancellations.

What To Do

Manage via proxies

Focus on high-wait zones identified through time/area analysis. Better ETAs, proactive driver positioning, and improved data collection.

KEY FINDING RECOMMENDATION

Waiting Time Effects Are Real But Contextual

Longer waits correlate with higher cancellations and lower ratings — best observed through time-of-day and area-level patterns. High-wait zones overlap with high-cancellation zones (Areas 2, 6, 11, 17, 35). Action: Pre-position drivers before peak hours in congested zones, provide realistic ETAs with live updates, and invest in better VTAT/CTAT data collection for future modeling.

Fare Modeling Attempt

Testing whether ride fares can be predicted from publicly available data alone — and being honest about the limitations.

Actual vs Predicted Fare (Ridge Regression, R² ≈ 0)
LIMITATION

Fare Prediction Is Not Feasible

Ridge Regression using distance, hour, and vehicle type achieves near-zero R². Fares depend on dynamic factors absent from this dataset: surge pricing, real-time traffic, promotions, and route-specific variables.

Without surge, traffic, and promotions data, fare modeling is exploratory. The analysis rightly focuses on operational efficiency where patterns are clearer and actionable.

Strategic Action Plan

For Operations

Target high-volatility zones with dynamic driver incentives during peak hours. Improve ETA accuracy and driver positioning in congested areas. Pre-position drivers before known peak windows using demand forecasting.

For Product

Nudge riders toward digital payments through in-app incentives and default selection. Surface realistic wait times transparently. Investigate cash payment friction points in the UX flow.

For Drivers

Stabilize earnings in volatile areas through guaranteed minimums during peak windows. Provide area-specific earnings insights. Reduce idle time with smarter reassignment after cancellations.

Limitations & Future Scope

Secondary Dataset

No surge pricing, promotions, or routing logic. Fare dynamics cannot be fully explained without internal data.

Incomplete VTAT/CTAT Data

~33% missing. Results use proxies — indicative, not exhaustive.

Bangalore-Specific

Findings may not generalize to other cities or time periods.

Future Work

With richer data (driver shifts, real-time traffic), build causal models and design real-time optimization strategies.
