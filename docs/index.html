Dataset & Methodology

The Bengaluru Ola Booking Dataset contains 50,000 ride records with 21 columns covering booking status, timestamps, pickup/drop locations, vehicle type, payment method, fare, ride distance, ratings, and cancellation reasons.

Correlation Analysis

Correlation analysis reveals how key operational metrics interact. Hour of day and booking value show the clearest associations with ride completion.

Temporal Demand Patterns

Ride demand in Bangalore is strongly time-dependent. Hourly ride counts rise from early morning, peak during evening commute (6-10 PM), and drop sharply at night.

Problem 1: Cancellations & Driver Earnings Volatility

How do ride cancellations affect drivers' earning stability across different times and areas?

Key Finding

Cancellations Are Concentrated

Cancellation rates spike during peak hours (evening 6-10 PM) and concentrate in high-density pickup zones. These same areas exhibit the highest volatility in driver take-home earnings.

Recommendation

Targeted Driver Incentives

Introduce dynamic driver incentives in high-cancellation zones during peak hours. Improve driver positioning and ETA accuracy to reduce rider cancellations.

Problem 2: Payment Methods & Ride Completion

How do different payment methods affect ride completion, cancellations, and customer experience?

Key Finding

Digital Payments Perform Better

Digital payments (UPI, wallets, cards) associate with higher completion rates and more consistent ratings compared to cash-based rides.

Recommendation

Nudge Toward Digital Payments

Encourage riders toward digital payment methods through in-app incentives, loyalty rewards, or default UPI/wallet selection at booking time.

Problem 3: Waiting Time & Service Quality

How do waiting times (VTAT/CTAT) affect cancellations and customer ratings?

Key Finding

Waiting Time Impacts Quality

Longer VTAT/CTAT correlates with higher cancellation risk and lower customer ratings, especially in congested areas during peak hours.

Recommendation

Manage Wait Times Proactively

Pre-position drivers before known peaks in high-wait zones. Provide riders with more accurate ETAs and live driver-location updates.

Fare Modeling

An exploratory fare prediction model using Ridge Regression demonstrates that accurate fare prediction is not feasible with the publicly available dataset.

Limitation

R² ≈ 0 — Fare Prediction Not Feasible

Ridge Regression achieves near-zero R², confirming that robust fare prediction requires real-time supply/demand data, surge multipliers, and traffic information.

Findings & Recommendations

Key Finding 1

Cancellations & Earnings

Cancellation rates concentrate in peak hours and high-density zones. These pockets exhibit the highest volatility in driver earnings.

Key Finding 2

Payment Methods

Digital payments (UPI, wallets) associate with higher completion rates and more consistent ratings compared to cash rides.

Key Finding 3

Waiting Time & Quality

Longer VTAT/CTAT correlates with higher cancellation risk and lower ratings, especially in congested peak-hour areas.

Operations

Target high-volatility zones with dynamic driver incentives during peak hours. Improve ETA accuracy.

Product

Nudge riders toward digital payments. Surface estimated wait times transparently.

Drivers

Stabilize earnings through guaranteed minimums during peak windows. Provide area-specific insights.

Limitations

  • Secondary dataset — no access to Ola's surge pricing, promotions, or routing logic
  • VTAT/CTAT fields missing for a significant portion of rides
  • Fare modeling limited by absence of contextual pricing variables
  • Geographic scope specific to Bangalore; may not generalize
