Step 1

Correlation Analysis

Understanding how operational metrics interact with each other and with ride success.

🔗 Correlation Heatmap
What We Found

Weak Correlations, Clear Patterns

No single metric strongly predicts ride success. Hour of day and booking value show the clearest (but still modest) associations with completion outcomes. Ratings cluster tightly around 4.0 with little variance — suggesting they're influenced by factors beyond the dataset.

💡 Key insight: Temporal patterns (hour, day) matter more than any single operational metric for predicting ride outcomes.
Step 2

Temporal & Spatial Demand Patterns

How does Ola ride demand vary across time and locations in Bangalore?

What We Found

Evening Peaks Drive Cancellations

Demand is lowest midnight-6 AM, rises from 7 AM, and peaks sharply 6-10 PM. Cancellation rates spike during these same evening windows — riders are more likely to cancel when wait times are uncertain and traffic congestion is worst.

💡 The evening peak (6-10 PM) is both the highest-demand and highest-cancellation period — a critical window for intervention.
Problem 1

Cancellations & Driver Earnings Volatility

How do ride cancellations and incomplete trips affect drivers' ability to earn steadily across different times and areas?

🔍 What we Analyzed

Cancellation patterns by area & hour

Examined cancellation rates across 45+ pickup areas and 24 hours. Compared earnings volatility in high vs low cancellation zones.

📊 What we Found

Cancellations cluster in hotspot areas

Areas like Area-27, 36, 37 show 35-37% cancellation rates. These same zones have the most volatile driver earnings — high income per trip but long idle periods between.

💡 What to Do

Dynamic driver incentives in hotspots

Introduce minimum earnings guarantees in high-cancellation zones during peak hours. Improve ETA accuracy to reduce rider frustration cancellations.

Key Finding

Cancellations Are Concentrated, Not Random

Cancellations spike during peak hours (evening 6-10 PM) and concentrate in specific high-density pickup zones (Areas 2, 6, 11, 17, 35, 27, 36, 37). These same pockets exhibit the highest volatility in driver take-home earnings — creating a double penalty where drivers face both frequent cancellations and unpredictable income.

Recommendation

Targeted Interventions for High-Volatility Zones

1. Driver incentives: Introduce minimum trip guarantees or short bonus schemes in high-cancellation, high-volatility pockets so drivers aren't penalized for serving difficult zones. 2. Smarter allocation: Strengthen matching logic — faster reassignment when a rider cancels, better ETAs, proactive driver placement before peak demand. 3. Rider communication: Add clearer wait-time messages before confirming booking in hotspots to discourage last-minute cancellations.

Problem 2

Payment Methods & Ride Completion

How do different payment methods affect ride completion, cancellations, and customer experience?

🔍 What we Analyzed

Payment method impact on completion

Compared completion rates, cancellation rates, and average ratings across Cash, UPI, Card, and Wallet payment methods.

📊 What we Found

Digital payments perform better

UPI and Wallet payments show higher completion rates and more consistent ratings. Cash transactions have slightly higher cancellation rates and more rating variability.

💡 What to Do

Nudge toward digital payments

Offer small discounts or loyalty points for UPI/Wallet payments. Default to digital payment in the app. Investigate cash ride friction points.

Key Finding

Payment Choice Matters for Completion

Digital payments (UPI, cards, wallets) associate with higher completion rates compared to cash. While all methods show generally high completion (~67%), cash transactions have a slightly larger share of cancellations. Customer ratings remain close to 4.0 across all methods with only minor variation.

Recommendation

Encourage Reliable Payment Methods

1. Incentivize digital: Small loyalty rewards, default UPI/wallet selection, or gentle prompts at booking time. 2. Monitor cash friction: Investigate whether payment flow issues (failed transactions, end-of-trip confusion) contribute to cash ride cancellations. 3. Targeted experiments: In cash-dominant areas with high cancellations, test digital-only offers or simplified digital onboarding.

Problem 3

Waiting Time & Service Quality

How do waiting times (VTAT/CTAT) affect cancellations and customer satisfaction?

🔍 What we Analyzed

VTAT patterns and rating correlation

Analyzed the distribution of VTAT (Vehicle Turnaround Time) for successful bookings and its relationship with customer ratings.

📊 What we Found

Weak direct VTAT-rating link

VTAT varies widely (1-20 min). Correlation with ratings is near zero — waiting-time effects show up more clearly in hourly/area patterns than in direct VTAT comparison.

💡 What to Do

Manage wait times via area/hour proxies

Focus on high-wait, low-rating zones identified through time/area analysis. Better ETAs and proactive driver positioning in congested zones.

📊 VTAT Distribution
⭐ Avg Customer Rating by VTAT Bin
Key Finding

Waiting Time Effects Are Contextual

Longer effective waiting times associate with higher cancellations and lower ratings — but the effect is best observed through time-of-day and area-level patterns rather than raw VTAT alone. High-wait zones overlap with high-cancellation zones identified in Problem 1, suggesting a localized service quality issue.

Recommendation

Reduce Friction in High-Wait Zones

1. Driver positioning: Pre-position drivers before known peak hours in congested pickup areas. 2. ETA transparency: Give riders realistic ETAs and live driver-location updates during peak windows. 3. Data investment: Improve VTAT/CTAT data collection to build precise models of rider patience thresholds.

Limitation

Fare Prediction Not Feasible (R² ≈ 0)

Ridge Regression using distance, hour, and vehicle type achieves near-zero R². Fares depend on dynamic factors absent from this dataset: surge pricing, real-time traffic, promotional discounts, and route-specific variables.

⚠️ Without access to Ola's internal surge and pricing data, fare modeling is exploratory at best. The analysis rightly focuses on operational efficiency where patterns are clearer.

🎯 Strategic Action Plan

⚙️

For Operations

Target high-volatility zones with dynamic driver incentives during peak hours. Improve ETA accuracy and driver positioning in congested pickup areas. Use demand forecasting to pre-position drivers before known peak windows.

📱

For Product

Nudge riders toward digital payment methods through in-app incentives. Surface estimated wait times more transparently to manage rider expectations. Investigate cash payment friction points in the UX flow.

👨‍✈️

For Drivers

Stabilize earnings in volatile areas through guaranteed minimums during identified peak windows. Provide area-specific earnings insights to help drivers choose optimal zones. Reduce idle time through smarter reassignment after cancellations.

Scope

Limitations & Future Work

Being transparent about what this analysis can and cannot tell us.

📡

Secondary Dataset Constraints

No access to Ola's internal surge multipliers, promotional discounts, or real-time routing algorithms. Certain behaviors (especially fare dynamics) cannot be fully explained without this data.

⏱️

Incomplete Turnaround-Time Data

VTAT/CTAT fields are missing for ~33% of rides. Analysis uses a cleaner subset and time-of-day/area proxies — results should be treated as indicative, not exhaustive.

📉

Fare Prediction Not Possible

Exploratory fare models consistently underperform a simple mean baseline. Robust prediction requires surge, traffic, promotions, and route data not available here.

📍

Geographic & Temporal Scope

Findings are specific to Ola's Bangalore operations during the dataset's time period. Different cities, time ranges, or newer pricing policies may produce different patterns.

🔬

Future Work

With access to richer operational logs (real wait times, driver shift schedules, route/traffic data), future studies could build causal models, evaluate specific interventions, and design real-time optimization strategies for driver deployment and pricing.
